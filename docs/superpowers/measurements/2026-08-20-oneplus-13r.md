# Throughput measurements — OnePlus 13R (SM8650)

Device: OnePlus 13R, chipset SM8650, 11.0 GB total RAM, 8 cores.
Date: 2026-08-20, IST evening. WiFi connected, battery 97-98%, charging
throughout. Background apps present (WhatsApp x2, LinkedIn, OLX, YouTube),
so figures reflect real mixed-load conditions rather than an idle benchmark
rig.

All measurements taken via the app's own telemetry chip (top-right corner
during GENERATING state), reading it 2-4 times per generation to see the
in-flight number, not just a single sample.

Conditions recorded with `adb shell dumpsys battery` (temperature, in
tenths of a degree C) and `adb shell cat /proc/meminfo` (MemAvailable) before
and after each generation, plus `adb shell dumpsys thermalservice` for
per-component thermal status (all readings stayed at `mStatus=0`, i.e.
NONE/nominal, throughout — CPU core temps around 72-90 C are normal for this
SoC under sustained load and did not trigger thermal throttling states).

## Prompt used

"Explain how a neural network learns, in about five sentences." and two
similar five-sentence-answer prompts ("Describe the water cycle...",
"Explain what makes a good password...") — chosen to produce a
paragraph-length answer (100-200 tokens) so the measurement reflects
steady-state decode speed rather than a one-word reply dominated by prompt
processing.

## App bug noted, not investigated

During Laghu testing and one Tvara++ generation, the app occasionally
returned to STANDBY (or kept incrementing the tok/s chip indefinitely) with
no visible response text, despite the telemetry chip showing live,
plausible tok/s values throughout. This looks like a text-rendering/stream
bug independent of the inference engine (the chip is clearly tracking real
generation), most reproducible on the second message in the same
conversation. Out of scope for this benchmarking task — flagging here so it
is not mistaken for a throughput anomaly.

## Scaling rule derived

Two directly measured anchors:

- Laghu: 0.6B params, Q8_0 (~8.5 bits/weight) → 6.5 tok/s
- Tvara++: ~2.0B params, Q6_K (~6.6 bits/weight) → 4.0 tok/s

**Quantization factor**: assumed proportional to 1 / bits-per-weight
(memory-bandwidth-bound decode on ARM). Approximate GGUF bits-per-weight:
Q4_K_M = 4.5, Q5_K_M = 5.5, Q6_K = 6.6, Q8_0 = 8.5. This shape is
consistent with llama.cpp community ARM benchmarks (quantization format
discussions in the ggml-org/llama.cpp repository), which report Q4_K_M at
roughly 1.5-2x the decode speed of Q6_K on ARM; our bits-per-weight ratio
(6.6/4.5 ≈ 1.47) sits at the low end of that documented range. **This factor
is inferred, not independently measured** — we only measured one quant
level per model.

**Parameter-count scaling**: normalizing both anchors to Q6-equivalent
speed (Laghu: 6.5 × 8.5/6.6 ≈ 8.37 tok/s at 0.6B) and fitting
`throughput ∝ params^-k` through the two points gives **k ≈ 0.6** — much
weaker than naive inverse-proportional (k=1) scaling. This is plausible for
memory-bandwidth-bound decode on a mobile SoC, where cache and bandwidth
effects soften the size penalty compared to a pure compute-bound model.

Formula: `tps(params, quant) = 8.37 * (params/0.6)^-0.6 * (6.6/bpw(quant))`

This reproduces the Tvara++ anchor (2.0B, Q6_K) as 4.07 tok/s against a
measured 4.0 tok/s — within 2%.
